# Writing Style

This aims to create prose that is honest, straightforward, clean, and entertaining.

## Touchstone authors

- **George Orwell** — _Politics and the English Language_, _Shooting an Elephant_, _Notes on Nationalism_. Prefer the active voice, short words, plain construction, and concrete nouns. Cut figures of speech you have seen in print. If a word can be cut, cut it.
- **Sol Stein** — _Stein on Writing_. Sentence-level triage. Every sentence earns its place. Weak intensifiers (very, really, actually, just, quite, somewhat) are usually deletable. Strong verbs replace weak verb + adverb. Filter phrases ("I think", "I noticed", "it seems") earn their keep only when flagging opinion deliberately.
- **Douglas Adams** — Absurdity delivered with bureaucratic flatness. The Vogons reading poetry, the towel, the ships hanging in the sky in much the same way that bricks don't. The absurd content does the work; the prose around it stays straight-faced.

## Core rules

Apply when drafting. No particular order.

1. **Plain over fancy word.** Anglo-Saxon over Latinate where the choice exists. _Use_ over _utilize_, _help_ over _facilitate_, _show_ over _demonstrate_, _build_ over _construct_. Exceptions exist when the Latinate word carries technical precision.
2. **Strong verb.** If the sentence rests on _is_, _has_, _does_, _goes_, ask whether a better verb exists. _Patches_ beats _is a patch on_. _Powers_ beats _is the engine of_. _Lands_ beats _is placed in_.
3. **Active voice unless passive earns it.** Passive earns its keep when the agent is unknown, irrelevant, or deliberately suppressed. Most of the time it is not.
4. **Cut weak intensifiers.** very, really, actually, just, quite, somewhat, indeed, rather. Can usually be replaced with a single word instead of intensifier + adjective (e.g., very fast becomes rapid, prompt, hurried, swift).
5. **Cut filler connectives.** _What I can say is that…_, _Let's walk through…_, _In other words…_, _It is important to note that…_. Cut them.
6. **Flag opinion.** Mark contested claims with _I think_, _I find_, _to me_, _read another way_. Asserting an opinion as fact reads as either lazy or arrogant.
7. **Cold open.** Start with the found object, the anecdote, or the assertion. _In this post I will…_, _Let me tell you about…_, _Recently I was thinking about…_ are throat-clearing.
8. **Register shifts at the section level.** Within a section, hold one tone (analytical, snarky, reflective). Across sections, contrast deliberately.
9. **Read aloud.** If you stumble, the reader will.

## Anti-patterns

Recurring tells of LLM-generated prose to avoid.

- **Negative parallelism.** _It's not X, it's Y._ _Not just X, but Y._ With or without the em dash.
- **The inversion.** _X did not Y. X did Z._ Two-sentence reframe deployed for punch. Cousin of negative parallelism.
- **Overused vocabulary.** _delve_, _dive into_, _tapestry_, _kaleidoscope_, _intricate_, _multifaceted_, _nuanced_, _landscape_ (as metaphor), _robust_, _leverage_, _utilize_, _facilitate_, _streamline_, _comprehensive_, _crucial_, _essential_, _pivotal_, _paramount_, _endeavor_, _foster_, _harness_.
- **Bold-first bullet lists.** Every bullet opens with a bolded label and a colon.
- **Burstiness.** Every sentence in the 17–24 word band.
- **Staccato sentence runs.** _It works. It always works. Until it doesn't._
- **Sentence-opener concentration.** Many consecutive sentences starting with _The_, _This_, _It_, or _In_.
- **Affirming tags.** _And that's real_, _And that matters_, _And it is not small_, _And that's the point_, _And that's not nothing_, _And that's by design_.
- **Gerund fragment litany.** _building things, learning the tools, making yourself…_ Long strings of _-ing_ phrases.
- **Coined metaphors.** _seam_, _cut_, _safe-cut_, _surface area_, _load-bearing_.
- **Hedged assertions stacked.** _I think it might possibly be the case that…_
- **"It is X that Y" constructions.** _it is the case that_, _it is important that_, _it is interesting that_.
- **Adjectival pile-ups.** _careful, meticulous, painstakingly written_.
- **Compound-adjective tics.** _fast-paced_, _ever-evolving_, _well-crafted_, _cutting-edge_, _world-class_, _state-of-the-art_, _next-level_.
